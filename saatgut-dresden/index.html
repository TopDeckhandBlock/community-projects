We keep a seed library of about 120 old regional vegetable varieties. Gardeners borrow seeds in spring and return fresh seeds from their harvest in autumn, so the variety survives another year.

Why we exist

Most of what is sold in supermarkets grows from a handful of commercial varieties. Old regional varieties, bred for decades in Saxony, survive only if somebody keeps growing them. That is what the library does: the seeds circulate, the knowledge circulates with them, and every return makes the collection a little more robust.

What we do

Spring lending

March and April: members choose up to ten varieties, free of charge, with a short growing guide for each one.

Autumn seed return

September: gardeners bring back seeds from their best plants. We dry, clean and store them for next season.

Growing evenings

Once a month in the community room: seed cleaning, tomato pruning, saving seeds from beans and lettuce.

How it works

1

Pick your varieties

The catalogue lists taste, growing height, days to harvest and whether the variety is bee friendly.

2

Grow and observe

You get a small bag of seeds and a one-page guide. No fee, no obligations beyond the return.

3

Bring seeds back

At the autumn event you return seeds from your healthiest plants, so the next gardener can start.
